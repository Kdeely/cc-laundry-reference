    Program overview

    SSDC standard v1.6 (10/2/26) for the three main laundry washers at Chevy Chase Club: fifteen cycles, one program set delivered as .ctf files, three products through a Seko LS100. The pages below are the rules the cycles were built to, the LS100 formulas, the machine and dispenser settings assumed, and how each linen type is treated. "Today vs standard" has the cycle-time and workload comparison; "Machine profile" the per-machine settings. Select a cycle on the left for its steps beside today's three machines, its chemistry and its checkpoints.

    Program design rules how the SSDC standard was built, what it assumes, known linen issues, rewash proposal

    Chemistry. Three products on a shared Seko LS100: C1 built detergent (surfactant + alkali), C2 chlorine bleach, C3 sour/soft. No break product. Every chemical goes into a low fill (level 15); flushes and rinses are high (25). Bleach always has its own bath after the wash, never with detergent.

    Structure. Flush, high, 2:00, with the AFS pulse during the fill (2 s × cycle number) → wash, hot, low, C1 → (F&B: carryover bath, no new chemical) → (whites: bleach bath, C2) → two high rinses, stepping hot/warm to warm → sour/soft, cold, low, 4:00 → extract. A 0:30 drain-and-extract after the wash and after the bleach stands in for the SSI third rinse on most cycles: an extract sheds most of the free liquor, where a rinse only dilutes it, and saves about 3 minutes and a high fill. If residual chlorine or pH tests fail on a two-rinse cycle, add the third rinse back before changing anything else.

    Third rinse. Built into 5, 14 and 15 only: warm, high, 2:00, between Rinse 2 and the sour/soft bath. Rationale: these three carry the longest bleach baths (9–10 min) on unheated water, they are ironed, so any retained chlorine shows as yellowing on the chest, and they are about 5 loads a day, so the cost is about 3 min per load and 17 min a day across the room (times 56, 59 and 56). Each high rinse dilutes what the load holds by roughly 4–5×; two rinses after the extract land where three rinses after a plain drain would, so the third on these cycles is insurance, not a correction. Towels (3) stay at two: half the bleached loads, a short bleach bath, and the 9:00 extract and dryer handle residual odour better than a rinse. Sheets (1), uniforms (8), ½ white (11) and rags (6) stay at two with the Rinse 2 drain and damp-linen chlorine checks deciding.

    Fiber. Cotton (sheets, towels, cotton napkins): bleach bath ≤140°F, hard extract. Polyester table linen (4, 5, 14): cooldown to 120°F before the last hot bath drains, warm (not hot) rinses, medium extract, no spray rinses. Cycles 5, 14 and 15 are built safe for a cotton/poly mix until the manager settles the sorting question; if white cotton is split out, 15 can drop the cooldown and extract 9:00 high.

    Temperature. None of the three main washers has a heater (the preset data shows neither the electric nor steam option set on the 65s, and Main 1's heater box is unchecked). Every "heat to target" in the current programs does nothing, and the Hot/Warm/Cold globals only matter for the cooldown setpoint (keep 120°F). Bath temperature is set by the valves alone: hot fill = incoming hot water, warm = hot + cold. Measure the hot line at the first fill. Below about 130°F the bleach bath needs pH 9.0–9.5, which is what the drain-and-extract before the bleach bath is for; if the bath tests above that, add a hot high 2:00 flush between wash and bleach, as the pool formula does. The single biggest lever left for F&B stain removal is the water heater feeding the laundry: 140°F at the washer is the target if the plumbing allows a dedicated laundry setpoint.

    Signals. Washer S1 → LS100 Prewash input 1 (detergent), S2 → Main wash input 2 (bleach), S3 → Rinse input 5 (sour/soft), S4 → AFS input 6; signal common on 7/8. Every chemical supply step flushes the same single compartment, the one the LS100 tube enters; the AFS step flushes nothing (today the machines flush C1, C2 and C3 in turn, and Main 1 flushes C3 with hot water). The LS100 input names don't match the washer step names; detergent fires on "Prewash" during the wash bath. Supply signals are 0:30; the dose is the LS100 pump time, not the signal length. Each cycle fires each product once, so the LS100 never has to re-dose a pump within a formula.

    Sizes. Set the LS100 dosing unit to fl oz/cwt and each washer's size (45, 65, 65 lb). Every formula is then entered once in oz/cwt and the LS100 scales it per washer; the per-load ounces below are what the dispenser should deliver, for checking the calibration cup. One LS100 formula list (31 slots) is shared by all three machines, so tick all three under "Washer enabled" on formulas 1–15. The formula "Washer load %" field is statistics only and does not change the dose, which is why 10 and 11 carry their own halved oz/cwt.

    Dispenser behaviour that shapes the programs. The LS100 doses each phase once per formula (Latched mode discards a repeat trigger), so no cycle fires a product twice and the SSI two-bath white F&B is folded into one heavier wash plus a carryover. A step that is rapid-advanced past is a dose that never happens. After the last dose the washer is locked out for 1 minute; a cycle restarted inside that window loses its AFS pulse.

    Known issues with linens, and where the standard answers them.

    • Stains on colour tablecloths and napkins (the manager's main complaint). Causes found: one 13–15 min wash with no carryover, no heat anywhere, colour napkins with no cycle of their own, and half-weight dosing when they ran through cycle 10. Answer: cycle 4 extended to a 3:00 hot flush, 12:00 wash starting at 12 oz/cwt (the other F&B cycles start at 10) and a 7:00 carryover; cycle 10 given the poly cooldown and flagged for full-weight dosing; stains checked before the dryer. Stains that survive are the case for an oxygen bleach or a swatch-tested light chlorine bath.
    • Softener build-up and towel absorbency. Plain softener on every cycle for years, no sour. Answer: sour/soft combo dosed for pH, light on towels (3 oz/cwt), drop test on dried towels; a strip wash if they bead.
    • High pH into the ironer (yellowing, wrinkling, chest residue). Answer: sour on every ironed cycle, pH strip on the damp linen before the ironer, 6.0 or below.
    • Set creases in polyester. Spray rinses and a flat 5:00 extract on hot poly today. Answer: cooldown before the hot bath drains, warm rinses, about 180 G extract on 4, 5, 10, 14, 15.
    • Chlorine carried into the ironer and the dryer. Bleach bath followed by a drain only, two rinses, no sour. Answer: drain-and-extract after the bleach bath, chlorine checks at the bleach drain and on the damp linen.
    • Napkins out of cycle. Colour napkins run in 4 or 10; white cotton napkins ran a factory program on Main 1 and a rinse-only on Main 2. Answer: 14 and 15 corrected on all three machines; chart note that colour napkins go to 4.
    • Cycle times on Main 1 (35% of its cycles rapid-advanced). Answer: washes cut from 12–15 min to 8–10; Main 1 nets out flat in time.

    Rewash cycle (not in the set, decision pending). Pieces pulled before the dryer need somewhere to go. Proposed as cycle 16 on the 65s (off the posted chart) or in place of cycle 2 (colour sheets, 45 loads lifetime): hot high 3:00 flush with AFS → hot low 12:00 wash at the top of the heavy range → carryover 7:00 → for whites a 10:00 bleach bath → cooldown → two warm high rinses → sour/soft → extract by fibre. Two LS100 formulas if whites and colours both need it, or one colour-safe formula and a white-only chart rule.

    Changeover. Years of plain softener and no sour: expect softener release and greyer first washes, high linen pH and residue on the ironer chest. Take pH, chlorine and absorbency baselines before the switch and repeat them after one week.

    One list shared by all three washers (enable washers 1, 2 and 3 on each formula). Dosing unit fl oz/cwt; the LS100 scales by washer size. Delay 0 on every chemical. Formulas 16–31 empty. The washer selects the formula with the AFS pulse on trigger 6 (seconds ÷ 2).

    Configuration checklist machine and dispenser settings the programs assume; verify before Oct 6

    LS100 (SekoBlue). Global: dosing unit fl oz/cwt. Washers: three interfaces acquired in machine order; name, size 45 / 65 / 65 lb; mode Duration (AFS), time base 2 s; trigger polarity rising; trigger filter below 1 s (a 0:02 AFS pulse must survive it); Main wash double trigger enabled (so S2 lands on terminal 2 or 3); 1st phase auto start disabled; lockout 1 min; formula mode Latched; default formula none. Flushing time per washer: run the flush button with a coloured product in the line and set it to the time the colour reaches the drum plus 5 s. Chemicals: name, density, viscosity, tank size, label colour. Formulas 1–15 per the Chemistry panel, delay 0, washer enabled on all three; 13 empty; 16–31 empty.

    Trigger interface at each washer. S1 → 1 Prewash, S2 → 2 Main wash, S3 → 5 Rinse, S4 → 6 AFS, signal common → 7/8. UniMac supply outputs are 24 VAC from the machine transformer: set the input DIP to the 12–240 V range (not the 70 V filter) and leave the dry-contact jumper 9–10 and the 24 VDC 11–12 unused. Confirm on the 45 with the d020 water purge (all four outputs fire with the drain open) and on the 65s with Diagnostics → Output test.

    Chemical line into the washer. The venturi carries product in flush water; the line enters the chemical injection port with a check valve, and pressure at the port stays under 40 psi (IOM). Keep each supply step's compartment flush set as it is now; it is only doing anything if the line feeds a dispenser cup. Each LS100 dosing phase can fire up to four products on one washer signal, so the 65s' spare outputs (ES5–ES8) wired to the Spare input would allow a second detergent dose or a sour prewash later without touching the S1–S4 map.

    65s (UniLinc Touch). No heater option is set: leave the globals as they are (Hot 120, Warm 104, Cold 35, cooldown 120, levels 25/20/15); heat fields in steps are inert. Final extract speed: the printouts show "65RPM" on every cycle; read the actual value in the editor. Cotton cycles want the machine's high speed (300 G or more); ironed poly about 180 G; if the current value is a 650 rpm custom speed, towels, sheets and uniforms are under-extracted today. Agitate steps keep "Refill" on.

    Main 1 (M30+, 45 lb). "Heater machine" is unchecked and stays that way; its heat fields are inert too. Cooldown setpoint to 120. Its drains stay 0:30 and its extract speeds are Very low / Low / High / Very high / Ultra high: use Ultra high where the standard says max G, Low where it says about 180 G. Extract speed and agitation profile may apply per cycle rather than per step on this control.

    Water heater. Record the laundry hot-water setpoint and whether a mixing valve limits it. Every wash and bleach bath in this standard runs at that temperature.

    LDR-01 manual corrections. The CCC Laundry Reference Manual lists Washers 1 and 2 as the 65s and Washer 3 as the 45; the machines and audit reports say the opposite (Washer 1 = 45). Its supply-signal legend (S1 detergent, S2 alkali, S3 bleach, S4 sour) is the 5-product convention, not this site's. Fix both pages before a technician uses the manual to wire or program anything.

    Linen types what each fibre needs, and the extract it gets

    White cotton sheets (1). Alkali swells cotton and releases the soil; medium soil, 500–800 ppm. Any hot fill, hard extract. Chlorine up to 140°F at 100–150 ppm, bath pH never below 9. Ironed: no softener, pH 6.0–6.5, about 30% moisture. Grey = redeposition or softener film; yellow after ironing = pH high or chlorine left in.

    Cotton terry towels (3). Heaviest soil (body oil, sunscreen) and the most water retained: 1,000–2,000 ppm if soiled, chlorine 150–200 ppm, 9:00 extract. Softener kills absorbency, so C3 is light and dosed for pH 5.0–6.0. Wash apart from polyester; terry lint shows on napkins. Dry medium heat; over-drying makes them harsh.

    Polyester table linen, white and colour (4, 5, 14). Poly doesn't absorb water; food and lipstick oils sit on the fibre and need surfactant plus heat, which is why unheated water hurts F&B most and why these cycles carry a carryover bath and longer times. Thermoplastic: cool down before the hot bath drains, no cold spray on a hot load, extract about 180 G or pressure creases set that the ironer can't remove. No softener (film, masked absorbency). Colour gets no chlorine; whites can. Over-agitation pills it.

    Cotton table linen (15). Behaves like sheets: hard extract, no cooldown, chlorine capped at 140°F. Tannin stains (wine, coffee, tea) want an acid presoak the three products can't give. Heavy soil, 1,600–2,500 ppm. Runs on the poly settings until the manager splits cotton from poly.

    Blends: napkins and uniforms (7, 8, 14). Polyester rules for temperature, cooldown and extract; cotton rules for the bleach cap. Uniforms aren't ironed, so full sour/soft at pH 5.5–6.0. Chef coats carry grease: alkalinity toward the top of the range.

    Spreads and comforters (9). Bulky, light soil, often filled or coloured: medium level so they wet through, no chlorine, 8:00 extract, dried fully or they mildew.

    Cushion covers and shrinkables (12). Cold throughout, no chlorine, low extract, low-heat or air dry. Check the label before the first wash.

    Rags and mops (6). Grease and floor soil: 1,000–2,000 ppm, chlorine 200–400 ppm, no sour. Wash apart from everything else. Grease-laden rags left in a hot pile after the dryer can self-heat: cool them spread out.

    Extracts by type. Cotton that is dried (towels, rags, half-load whites): the machine's top speed, 5:00–9:00, because every point of moisture left is dryer time. Cotton that is ironed (sheets, cotton napkins once sorted): top speed, 6:00–8:00, then a short air tumble to ironer moisture. Polyester and blends (4, 5, 14, 15, 10, uniforms): about 180 G for 5:00; poly holds little water, and above roughly 200 G it creases. Shrinkables (12): low speed 4:00. Rinse & spin (13): 180 G, safe for whatever is in it. Intermediate extracts stay 0:30 at the machine's low speed. On the 65s top speed means the High/Very high settings (300–400 G); on Main 1 it means Ultra high (200 G), and its Low setting stands in for 180 G.

    Across all types. Sort by colour, then fibre. Load to rated weight. First wash of new linen: flush plus a high-surfactant wash, no bleach, to strip sizing. Over-chlorination and high pH with heat are what wear linen out early, which is why the bleach checkpoints matter more than any dose.

    Linen handling practices outside the washer, from the SSI manual and the LDR-01 stain reference

    Sorting. By colour, then by fibre: cotton table linen apart from polyester (bleach temperature, cooldown, extract all differ); reds and pinks never with whites. Load to the rated weight: an under-loaded drum is over-dosed per pound and over-agitated; an over-loaded one gets no mechanical action. Cycle 10 is where this goes wrong first.

    Before the dryer. Check F&B pieces for stains while damp and send them to a rewash; dryer heat sets food, wine and lipstick. Towels: feel for a slick hand (softener) and smell for chlorine. Sheets and table linen: pH strip on a damp piece, 6.5 or below.

    Dryer. Medium heat 50–60 min, or high 30–40 and add in 10-minute steps; scorched linen is lost. Towels need longer than sheets. Air-tumble ironed linen only enough to reach ironer moisture.

    Ironer. No softener on anything ironed (the combo on C3 is dosed for pH, not softness; a straight sour on a 4th inlet is the clean fix). Linen pH 6.0 or below into the ironer: above that, yellow spots after ironing, wrinkling, chest rust. Residual moisture about 30% by the weigh test. Keep the chest clean of softener film during the changeover.

    Storage and handling. Dry fully before shelving (mildew shows as black or pink spots); wet linen off bare concrete; check carts and the ironer for rust sources if red-brown spots appear.

    Stains the three products can't solve. Sunscreen on towels (needs a sour prewash and an oxygen bleach), set wine, coffee and tea (acid presoak), rust (acid), colour F&B stains that survive the heavy wash (oxygen bleach at 150–160°F, or a 50–80 ppm chlorine bath on colourfast polyester after a swatch test). Each is a 4th-product or reclaim-formula conversation, not a dose change.
